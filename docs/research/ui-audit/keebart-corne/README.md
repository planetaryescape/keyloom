# Keebart Corne renders both encoder presses and its stepped case

These screenshots use Keebart's public factory keymap at `98c5a1181ff7db1f480f684f714126922d55b8f0`, imported into a scratch data directory. They contain no owner layout or photographs. The app is the real localhost server; its disconnected state is accurate for firmware without keyloom's build markers.

The capture covers 1440×900, 1366×768, 1280×577 and 390×844 in both themes, with the case and caps pinned black. The drawing was compared with the owner's five supplied desk photos: black stepped case, a separate plate rim, three thumb keys per side, two tall inner thumb caps, one encoder above an extra inner key on each side, and no displays. The first review found the inner thumb caps protruding past the case; the outline was extended before these captures.

All caps now sit inside their half's case, the halves remain separate, and the phone shows one half at a time. The encoder presses remain buttons with the existing key-editor behavior and focus handling. Their circular face and concentric knurl distinguish them from regular caps. The product drawing omits the separate desk tenting stands, cables, keycap manufacturer's legends and perspective from the photographs. It shows the configured bindings instead.

The saved screenshot names identify the theme and viewport. Hardware typing and rotary direction still require physical acceptance; a screenshot does not establish either.
