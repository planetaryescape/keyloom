# S53: does replaying a recording through the emulators reproduce what the keyboard did?

Two halves. The emulator half is done and recorded below. The hardware half, a real keyboard's output against its own replay, is a runbook for the orchestrator with BK, and needs S50 and S51 first.

## Half 1: an emulator-generated typing, over the wire and back (2026-10-16, no hardware)

`test/lab/emulator.docker.test.ts` builds a passage's worth of typing with a seeded synthetic person (30 events: taps about 90 ms long every 140 ms, and holds of a mod-tap key with a partner key pressed inside it), runs it through the real firmware logic to get what the layout does with it (the stand-in for what a keyboard sent), then sends the same events through the stream's own wire format and the host reader:

- Moonlander: events become raw-HID reports (`typing-stream.ts` `encodeEventsReport`), a fake keyboard hands them to `startQmkCapture`, and the trace that comes out is prepared the way the lab prepares one (`prepareEvents`: sorted, started at zero, whole milliseconds, long pauses shortened) and replayed through QMK's own core in Docker.
- Glove80: events become `E <position> <1|0> <ms> <source>` lines, split into half-line pieces on the way, with the right half's keys as source 0 and the left's as 255, read by `startZmkCapture` and replayed through ZMK's own build in Docker.

Result, from `evidence/rung22/lab-qmk-real.log` and `lab-zmk-real.log`:

- The trace that comes out of the wire, once prepared, equals the original events, event for event.
- The replayed reports equal the reports the firmware gave for the original events, report for report: 30 events and 30 reports on QMK (text `"sd fj sd as"`), 30 and 30 on ZMK (text `"sd fj sdsk asfj"`).
- `analyse` with the recorded text finds 0 differing characters between what the "keyboard" typed and what the replay types.
- On the same two cores, a recording built to misfire (S held 260 ms with D pressed 150 ms in, four times) is classified as four holds where a tap was meant; the search's best change (QMK: tapping term 200 to 280 ms; ZMK: a positional hold, split by hand) is applied and the same keys replayed on the real core show none.

What the real cores showed that a model would not have:

- QMK treats a timer of zero as "not started". A recording that begins with S and D, which BK's layout makes a Shift combo, lost both letters of its first roll and none of the later ones, because the harness's clock starts at zero. The lab's replays now start the harness 100 ms early (`leadMs`) and take it off the reports. A real keyboard that has been on for a while is never at zero, except once every 65 seconds of its 16-bit timer, which is a reason to keep the lead rather than a reason for it to matter.
- ZMK's `hold-trigger-on-release` waits for the other key's release before it looks at its position, but a hold-preferred key is decided when the other key goes down, so the positional rule saw nothing and the hold stood. The split profiles set it only for flavors that wait for a release.
- The synthetic person's hold step on the Glove80 (S held 420 ms, K pressed 250 ms in) came out as the letters `sk`, not a hold: the test keymap's home row mods are balanced with a positional trigger on release and a 280 ms term, and K is released after the term. The lab reports that as a tap where a hold was meant.

## Half 2: a real keyboard against its own replay (needs BK)

Prerequisites: S50 (Moonlander) or S51 (Glove80) passed; the keyboard runs a keyloom build with the stream.

1. Use the text-only passage so a hold's Option or Command character does not enter into it:

   ```sh
   keyloom lab start <layout> --passage home-row-rolls
   ```

   Type the passage as you would, at your normal speed, and make the mistakes you make. Ctrl-C when done.

2. `keyloom lab analyse <session> --no-propose`. Read the line `Replay check: your keyboard typed N characters here and the replay typed M; K differ.`

3. Pass if K is at most 2% of N. A larger K means the replay does not type what the keyboard did, and the suggestions cannot be trusted; the analysis already says so in a note when K is over 10%.

4. Make it misfire on purpose: set the home row profile's tapping term to 120 ms (`keyloom change` or the editor), sync, and repeat steps 1 to 3. Pass if K is still at most 2% and `Holds where a tap was meant` is above zero: the replay reproduced the keyboard's misfires, not only its clean typing.

5. If K is large, record the session ID and keep the session. Its `typed_text` and `events` are in `typing_sessions` in keyloom's database; a developer replays a stretch of the events with `keyloom emulate --script` and compares with `typed_text` to find where the two part.
