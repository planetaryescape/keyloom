# A UF2 copy that ends with ENXIO is taken as done, even when it was cut short

When keyloom copies a UF2 image to a Glove80 half's boot drive, a copy that ends with `ENXIO` while the drive unmounts counts as complete. That's because the bootloader unmounts the drive once it has every block (`src/firmware/toolchain/uf2.ts`).

On 2026-10-22 the left half's copy ended that way during a hands-free reflash (`fl_01M5H5PV3KVFCCR64T69PA472S`, published v0.49.0). The half then never came back on USB. It returned only after BK started it in its bootloader by hand (Magic + E at power-on), when a rewrite verified (`fl_01M5HD0018N66TVKPFR5DSJW1E`). So that copy was most likely truncated: the drive went away before the last blocks were written.

The post-flash check caught it, so nothing was reported as verified. But the message ("didn't come back within 45 seconds … check its USB cable") points at the cable, when the half actually needs its bootloader key and a rewrite.

Fix:
- Treat an `ENXIO`-ended copy as unconfirmed, not done.
- When the half then doesn't come back, say that it may have incomplete firmware, and give that half's bootloader instruction so the user can rewrite it. The rewrite needs no other recovery.
- Add a test with a fake volume that unmounts mid-copy.

Status: fixed on PR 158's branch, `feat/split-flash-rollback` (D-437). The orchestrator adds the fixing SHA. A half that doesn't come back is now told its firmware may be incomplete and given its own bootloader instruction to rewrite it, before the cable advice. The copy itself still counts an ENXIO-plus-unmount ending as done, because on 2026-10-16 exactly that ending was a complete write; the post-flash check is what tells the two apart.


Split-flash recovery is implemented on PR 158's branch (D-437). Last-good promotion waits for both halves to verify. If a later half fails, the verified halves are restored to their saved build; an absent or unknown failed half then gets its own bootloader instruction, saved image and verification. Recovery stays a failed flash and sync, with both final states in the error and log. Without a usable saved image, or after a restore failure, the error gives the manual recovery step. Recovery writes a shared drive only after keyloom restarts the specific target and observes its drive appear, while its peers report running marked firmware. Normal flashing and Flash again also accept a manually opened drive appearing after the prompt, but immediately before writing every shared-label peer must visibly run firmware on USB, either keyloom's or other firmware. An absent peer, a peer in its bootloader, or a drive already mounted before the prompt refuses the write. A delayed peer drive cannot bypass the check. Keyloom cannot tell which half owns that drive: the peer must be running, or recovered first on its own with only that half connected. Both halves must run firmware before retrying; wait for each prompt in the recipe's original order. Unmarked peers without known running USB IDs cannot be confirmed. Boards without hands-free hooks keep their existing manual flow. Missing halves keep the stored failure visible. A later successful flash of the failed revision or a descendant, corroborated by every expected half running its build, supersedes the failure for status and subsequent auto-sync, preserving the failed sync row as history. This also applies to QMK and single-step boards. Reboot-only writes no drive, so it does not require a shared-drive peer marker.
